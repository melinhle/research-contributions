The project

Author order tells only part of the story.

As LIS scholarship becomes more collaborative, an author list alone cannot explain each person’s contribution. Our research examines how contribution statements are used—and what they reveal about the distribution of research labour.

Two connected papers investigate adoption across LIS journals and gender patterns in contributor roles. A further conference presentation focuses on health librarianship.

Two connected studies

How is research credit made visible?

01

Are contribution statements being used?

We examined first-issue articles from 2023 and 2024 and journal author guidelines to understand the frequency, format, and enforcement of contribution statements.

Among 129 actively publishing LIS journals, eight required contribution statements. Compliance varied, with only the Journal of the Medical Library Association reaching 100% in the sample.

02

What do contributor roles reveal about gender?

Using CRediT statements from LIS articles published in the first issues of 2023 and 2024, we examined the frequency of contributor roles and their distribution by gender.

The study describes a collaborative writing culture alongside gender differences in research labour, including leadership, administration, and data curation.

Women accounted for 45.15% and men for 54.85% of the gender-classified authorship reported in the study. These figures describe the study sample, rather than the LIS workforce.

Methods, scope, and interpretation

The adoption paper reports 2,590 screened records, 1,748 research or scholarly articles, and 161 articles with contribution statements. Its headline 6% uses 2,590 as the denominator; 161 of 1,748 is approximately 9.2%. We present the counts here to make that distinction explicit.

The studies sample first issues, rather than all articles published during each year. Journal selection and language eligibility also shape coverage. Contribution statements describe credited work, which may differ from work actually performed.

The gender analysis used name-based inference and checks of publicly available information; gender was not self-reported. The study could not identify gender-diverse authors. Binary findings cannot represent the full diversity of researchers’ identities.

Consult each paper for its full methods, analysis, and limitations. The ACRL poster represents an earlier analysis and should not be treated as interchangeable with the final papers.

Understanding contributorship

What is CRediT?

The Contributor Roles Taxonomy describes 14 types of contribution to research. It provides a shared vocabulary for explaining who did what, from developing the research question to curating data and preparing a manuscript.

From evidence to practice

Make recognition part of the research process.

For researchers

  • Discuss contributions early and revisit roles throughout a project.
  • Use a structured contribution statement, including when a journal does not require one.
  • Recognize the full range of research work, beyond writing.

For journal editors

  • Require contribution statements for research articles.
  • Apply requirements consistently during submission and publication.
  • Make statements visible and accommodate contributions that a taxonomy may not capture.

Recommendations synthesized from the supplied papers and ICML presentation.
